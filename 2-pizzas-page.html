What is the Two Pizza Rule?

The Two Pizza Rule is a simple yet powerful guideline created by Amazon founder Jeff Bezos. It states that teams should be small enough to be fed by two pizzas - typically 6-8 people.

🍕 + 🍕 = 👥 Perfect Team Size
6-8 People

💡 Key Insight: Smaller teams communicate better, make decisions faster, and are more innovative than large, bureaucratic groups!

Why It Works for Software Teams

🚀 Communication Efficiency

As teams grow larger, communication complexity grows exponentially. With n people, there are n(n-1)/2 potential communication channels. A 6-person team has 15 channels, while a 12-person team has 66!

⚡ Faster Decision Making

Smaller teams can reach consensus quickly without lengthy meetings or complex approval processes. Everyone's voice can be heard and decisions can be made in real-time.

🎯 Clear Accountability

In a small team, individual contributions are more visible, leading to higher accountability and ownership of work quality.

How to Implement the Two Pizza Rule

🔍 Audit Your Current Teams

Look at your existing development teams. Are any larger than 8 people? Consider splitting them into smaller, focused units.

🎯 Define Clear Ownership

Each small team should own specific features, services, or products. This creates clear boundaries and reduces dependencies.

📊 Measure Success

Track metrics like deployment frequency, lead time, and team satisfaction to validate that smaller teams are performing better.

🍕 Pro Tip: If you need to book a conference room larger than what fits 8 people comfortably, your team might be too big!

When to Break the Rule

While the two pizza rule is excellent guidance, there are exceptions:

  • Crisis situations - Sometimes you need all hands on deck
  • Knowledge transfer - Temporary larger groups for training
  • Cross-functional alignment - Occasional larger meetings for coordination
  • Company-wide initiatives - Strategic planning sessions

Remember: Two pizzas, endless possibilities! 🍕🍕
